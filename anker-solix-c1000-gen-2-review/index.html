Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 Review: Is a 1,024Wh Power Station Enough for Backup?

The Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 is a compact 1kWh-class portable power station with high AC output, fast recharge, and enough battery for selected essentials — as long as you do the runtime math before buying.

Quick verdict

The Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 is one of the more interesting 1kWh-class power stations because it combines a 1,024Wh LFP battery with 2,000W AC output, 3,000W peak output, 600W max solar input, and very fast AC recharge. That makes it a strong fit for camping, RV weekends, outage essentials, portable work, and small cabin loads.

It is not a whole-home backup system. Think refrigerator cycling, router, lights, fan, laptop, camera batteries, phone charging, and short appliance use — not central AC, electric heat, water heaters, or days of full household power.

Key specs to know

  • Battery capacity: 1,024Wh
  • AC output: 2,000W rated output, 3,000W peak
  • Solar input: 600W max solar input
  • AC recharge: full recharge in about 49 minutes using UltraFast mode
  • Battery chemistry: LFP / LiFePO₄, rated for 4,000 cycles to at least 80% capacity
  • UPS switchover: 10 ms
  • Weight: about 24.9 lb
  • Ports: 10 output ports

What can the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 realistically run?

The C1000 Gen 2 stores 1,024Wh. For AC loads, it is safer to plan around less than the full number because the inverter uses some power. A practical planning range is roughly 820–900 usable watt-hours for many AC loads, depending on the appliance and conditions.

  • 50W portable fridge: roughly 16–18 hours if it pulled 50W continuously; often longer if it cycles on and off.
  • 85W TV: roughly 9–10 hours after inverter losses.
  • 40W fan: roughly 20–22 hours.
  • 60W laptop charger: roughly 13–15 hours of charging time.
  • 600W microwave: possible for short bursts, but it drains the battery quickly.
  • 1,500W heater or hair dryer: the output may handle the load, but runtime will be short.

The output rating tells you what it can power at once. The watt-hours tell you how long it may last. Confusing those two numbers is how people buy the wrong power station.

Best use cases

  • Short outage backup: refrigerator cycling, internet, lights, phones, and a fan.
  • Camping and RV trips: device charging, portable fridge, lighting, small kitchen appliances, and short high-watt tasks.
  • Portable work power: laptops, camera batteries, networking gear, small tools, and field electronics.
  • Beginner solar generator setup: pair it with compatible portable panels up to the 600W solar input limit.

Where I would be careful

  • Do not treat 2,000W output like unlimited runtime. A 1,500W load can empty a 1kWh battery fast.
  • Check solar compatibility before buying panels. The station accepts up to 600W solar input, with voltage/current limits that must be respected.
  • Do not oversell UPS mode. A 10 ms switchover is useful for some electronics, but critical medical or server setups still deserve careful compatibility testing.
  • Watch the deal price. In this size class, the best choice often depends on current pricing versus BLUETTI, EcoFlow, Jackery, and other 1kWh competitors.

C1000 Gen 2 vs BLUETTI AC180 and Elite 100 V2

The C1000 Gen 2 is worth comparing against the BLUETTI AC180 and BLUETTI Elite 100 V2. The Anker unit has a strong output rating for the size, fast AC recharge, and a light body for a 1kWh-class station. The BLUETTI AC180 has slightly more battery capacity at 1,152Wh, while the Elite 100 V2 is another modern lightweight option with high solar input.

If prices are close, the C1000 Gen 2 is especially appealing for buyers who care about output power, recharge speed, portability, and Anker’s app ecosystem. If the AC180 is deeply discounted, the extra capacity may matter more than the spec-sheet speed.

Should you buy the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2?

Buy it if you want a compact, high-output 1kWh power station for outage essentials, camping, RV weekends, small cabin loads, or portable work. It is a strong “first serious station” if your loads fit the 1kWh class.

Skip it if your real goal is multi-day home backup, whole-home power, central air conditioning, electric heat, or large off-grid living. For those jobs, look at a 2kWh-class station like the Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2 or a larger expandable system.
