About SolarKitGuide

Solar decisions should feel clearer, not more confusing.

SolarKitGuide was created for people who are starting the journey toward adding solar to a home, cabin, RV, shed, backup system, or other off-grid setup — and want a simpler way to understand what they actually need.

Why we built this site

We are enthusiastic about solar systems, but we also know how frustrating it can be to find clear, useful information when you are trying to make an educated decision about solar power components. Panels, batteries, inverters, charge controllers, system voltage, surge watts, daily energy use, backup loads — it can feel like you need to learn a new language before you can make a good decision.

SolarKitGuide grew out of that frustration. We wanted to create the kind of straightforward resource we wished was easier to find: practical calculators, plain-English explanations, and beginner-friendly guides that help people understand the major components, compare options more confidently, and avoid buying a kit that is too small, too expensive, or mismatched for the job.

Who SolarKitGuide is for

This site is especially for people who are just beginning to explore solar and want to confidently choose the components they need to accomplish their solar goals.

  • Homeowners trying to understand solar kit size, backup power, or battery storage.
  • Cabin, tiny home, shed, and workshop owners planning an off-grid or hybrid setup.
  • RV and camper owners who want to size panels, batteries, and inverters around real appliance loads.
  • DIY-minded buyers comparing solar generators, all-in-one kits, and component-based systems.

Our editorial approach

We write for clarity first. The calculators and guides on SolarKitGuide are designed to explain the assumptions behind the numbers, not just give a quick answer with no context.

  • Beginner-friendly: We avoid unnecessary jargon and explain key terms when they matter.
  • Practical: We focus on real decisions: panel watts, usable battery capacity, inverter size, surge loads, and system type.
  • Conservative where it counts: Solar sizing depends on location, weather, shade, appliance behavior, and installation details, so we prefer useful ranges over false precision.
  • Transparent: When we use affiliate links or earn commissions, we disclose that clearly. Affiliate relationships do not change our goal: helping readers make better solar decisions.

Important note

SolarKitGuide is an educational resource, not a substitute for a licensed electrician, engineer, installer, or local permitting authority. Use our calculators as a planning starting point, then verify your final design, code requirements, wiring, safety equipment, and installation details with qualified professionals.
