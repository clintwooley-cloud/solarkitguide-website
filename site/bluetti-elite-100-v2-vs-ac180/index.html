BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 vs AC180: Which 1kWh Power Station Makes More Sense?

The AC180 and Elite 100 V2 sit in the same general class, but they are not identical. The AC180 gives you a little more battery capacity; the Elite 100 V2 is lighter, newer, and accepts more solar input.

Short answer

Choose the BLUETTI AC180 if you want slightly more battery capacity and a proven 1,152Wh portable power station for general backup and camping use.

Choose the BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 if you want a lighter station, higher solar input, quieter operation under light loads, WiFi/Bluetooth control, and 10ms UPS-style switchover in a newer 1kWh-class design.

Spec comparison

  • Battery capacity: AC180 has 1,152Wh; Elite 100 V2 has 1,024Wh.
  • AC output: both are 1,800W class with 2,700W lifting/surge-style support for certain loads.
  • Solar input: AC180 supports 500W max; Elite 100 V2 supports 1,000W max.
  • Weight: AC180 is about 35.3 lb; Elite 100 V2 is about 25 lb.
  • Battery cycle rating: AC180 is listed at 3,500+ cycles; Elite 100 V2 is listed at 4,000+ cycles.
  • Connectivity: AC180 uses Bluetooth app control; Elite 100 V2 lists WiFi/Bluetooth.
  • UPS switchover: Elite 100 V2 lists 10ms; AC180 comparison data lists 20ms.

The real difference: battery capacity vs charging flexibility

The AC180 stores about 128Wh more energy than the Elite 100 V2. That is useful, but it is not a massive difference in real-world runtime. For AC loads after inverter losses, think of both as roughly “one useful kilowatt-hour” class stations.

The bigger practical difference is solar input and portability. The Elite 100 V2 can accept up to 1,000W of solar input, while the AC180 is capped at 500W. If you plan to recharge hard from panels during the day, the Elite 100 V2 has the stronger solar ceiling. If you mostly charge from the wall and want a sale-priced all-around unit, the AC180 may make more sense.

Runtime expectations

For planning, assume both stations provide less usable AC energy than their nameplate battery capacity because inverters use some energy. A rough planning range:

  • Elite 100 V2: about 820–900 usable watt-hours for many AC loads.
  • AC180: about 920–1,000 usable watt-hours for many AC loads.

That means either station can handle device charging, lights, fans, routers, laptops, and short appliance use. Neither is the right tool for long electric heating, whole-home backup, or extended air conditioner runtime.

Choose AC180 if...

  • You want a slightly larger battery in the same general class.
  • You find it at a better sale price.
  • You care more about wall charging and general backup than fast solar input.
  • You do not mind the heavier 35 lb weight.

Which one would I start with for SolarKitGuide readers?

For a first portable power station article, the AC180 is easier to explain because it is a broad, practical, middle-of-the-road unit. For buyers who care about weight and solar recharging, the Elite 100 V2 may be the more interesting pick.

My recommendation: compare current pricing. If the Elite 100 V2 is close in price to the AC180, its lighter weight and stronger solar input are compelling. If the AC180 is meaningfully cheaper, it remains a very sensible beginner power station.
