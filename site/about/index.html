Why we built this site

We are enthusiastic about solar systems, but we also know how frustrating it can be to find clear, useful information when you are trying to make an educated decision about solar power components. Panels, batteries, inverters, charge controllers, system voltage, surge watts, daily energy use, backup loads — it can feel like you need to learn a new language before you can make a good decision.

SolarKitGuide grew out of that frustration. We wanted to create the kind of straightforward resource we wished was easier to find: a free calculator to create a realistic starting point, plain-English explanations that make the numbers understandable, and equipment guides that help people compare options more confidently and avoid buying a kit that is too small, too expensive, or just wrong for the job.

Who SolarKitGuide is for

This site is especially for people who are just beginning to explore solar and want to confidently choose the components they need to accomplish their solar goals.

  • Homeowners trying to understand solar kit size, backup power, or battery storage.
  • Cabin, tiny home, shed, and workshop owners planning an off-grid or hybrid setup.
  • RV and camper owners who want to size panels, batteries, and inverters around real appliance loads.
  • DIY-minded buyers comparing solar generators, all-in-one kits, and component-based systems.

Our editorial policy

SolarKitGuide is built around a simple principle: sizing logic first, product recommendations second. We want readers to understand the job their equipment needs to do before comparing prices, bundles, product pages, or brands.

  • Beginner-friendly: We avoid unnecessary jargon and explain key terms when they matter.
  • Practical: We focus on real decisions: panel watts, usable battery capacity, inverter size, surge loads, system voltage, and system type.
  • Conservative where it counts: Solar sizing depends on location, weather, shade, appliance behavior, and installation details, so we prefer useful ranges over false precision.
  • Transparent: Affiliate relationships are disclosed clearly. Compensation may influence which retailers or products are linked, but it should not change the calculator math or the basic sizing guidance.
  • Correctable: If something is unclear, outdated, or wrong, we want to know. Corrections can be sent to hello@solarkitguide.com.

How we plan to evaluate equipment

As SolarKitGuide expands its retail equipment sections, the goal is to organize recommendations around use cases and system size instead of pushing a generic “best” product. A small RV battery setup, a refrigerator backup system, a cabin kit, and a whole-home hybrid system are very different buying decisions.

When we feature products or retailers, we prefer equipment and partners with clear specifications, realistic wattage and capacity claims, US availability, understandable warranties, reliable fulfillment, and product categories that match the calculator result. The ideal path is simple: size the job first, learn the tradeoffs second, then compare products in the right class.

Important safety note

SolarKitGuide is an educational resource, not a substitute for a licensed electrician, engineer, installer, or local permitting authority. Use our calculators as a planning starting point, then verify your final design, code requirements, wiring, overcurrent protection, disconnects, grounding, roof mounting, battery safety, and installation details with qualified professionals.
